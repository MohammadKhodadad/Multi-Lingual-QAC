## Blocking issues (MISMATCH / UNTRACEABLE numbers) — must fix

### B1 (MISMATCH). "English is the easiest target language" is false on its own data.
- **Where:** §Results "Retrieval is anisotropic" (line ~399): *"English is the easiest
  target."* Also inherited from chem-patents `EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY.md` headline #2 and from
  `round02_directional_clir.py` line 217.
- **What the data says:** the claim is generated by
  `round02` as `english_as_doc_target_meanrecall = mp["en"].mean() = 0.367` — but the code
  **never compares it to the other target columns.** Recomputing the pooled (8 reliable
  models) target-column means from `pair_recall.csv`:
  `en = 0.367`, **`fr = 0.375`**, `zh = 0.350`, `de = 0.309`. **French (0.375) is the
  easiest target, not English (0.367).** The effect is even stronger in the figure the
  paragraph actually shows: `cp_fig03_directional_clir_matrix.png` is the *embeddinggemma*
  matrix, whose en-target column averages **0.49** vs the fr-target column **0.56** — i.e.
  Figure 3 visually contradicts the sentence next to it.
- **Minimal fix:** either (a) drop "English is the easiest target" and replace with the
  defensible, traceable statement *"the hardest direction is en→de (R@10 = 0.12 pooled)
  and the most asymmetric pair is de↔zh (gap +0.23)"* (both verified, see table), or
  (b) restate as *"English and French are the two strongest target languages"* if a hub
  claim is wanted. Do **not** keep "English is the easiest target" as written.

### B2 (attribution imprecision — must fix wording). "even the best model reaches a cross-lingual RBO of only 0.39 … and 0.19 on the cross-lingual benchmark."
- **Where:** §Introduction (line ~98) and Conclusion.
- **What the data says:** On the alias benchmark, 0.39 = embeddinggemma (the headline best
  model) ✓. On the cross-lingual benchmark, the **0.19 ceiling belongs to granite-278m**
  (`round05.ceiling_model = "granite-278m"`, `ceiling_rbo = 0.1934`); the headline best
  model embeddinggemma is at **RBO = 0.154** (`headline_numbers.csv`). So "even the best
  model reaches … 0.19" conflates "best model" with "ceiling across models." The **value
  0.19 is correct as a ceiling**, but the attribution is wrong.
- **Minimal fix:** change to *"the best cross-lingual RBO any model achieves is 0.39
  (alias-graph) / 0.19 (cross-lingual)"* — i.e. say "best across models / ceiling," not
  "the best model." (The abstract already says "ceiling 0.39," which is fine; only the
  intro/conclusion "best model" phrasing needs this.)

### B3 (presentational confound — add one sentence). Figure 1 green bars (MoLIR) are a different population than the grey/red bars.
- **Where:** Fig 1 / `cp_fig01_clir_leaderboard.png` and the "home advantage" framing.
- **What the data says:** `round01` computes MoLIR@10 **only on the 57 original queries**
  (legend: "same-language gold, originals"), while overall-R@10 and CLIR@10 are over all
  137. So the green vs grey/red comparison mixes populations *in the figure*. **Good news:
  the home-advantage metric itself (+0.55) is NOT confounded** — `round01` lines 44–45
  compute it **paired within the 57 originals** (MoLIR−CLIR per query, each original has
  both a same- and a cross-language gold). So +0.55 is a clean paired contrast and is
  verified-correct.
- **Minimal fix:** one sentence in the Fig 1 caption / setup: *"MoLIR@10 is defined only on
  the 57 original queries (the only ones with a same-language gold); the home advantage is
  measured paired within those queries."* This pre-empts the reviewer who notes the green
  bar is a different N.

> Everything else below is verified-correct or a non-blocking threat.

## Design-soundness findings (per headline claim)

1. **"Average recall hides the collapse" / CLIR vs MoLIR (Fig 1–2).** The instrument is
   sound and the +0.55 home advantage is a **clean paired within-query contrast** (verified
   in code). The only soundness wrinkle is presentational (B3): the green MoLIR bar in Fig 1
   is the 57-original subpopulation. Add one clarifying sentence; the metric is fine.

2. **Directional CLIR matrix (Fig 3).** Two design facts the writer must respect:
   (a) the **per-cell N is tiny** — en→de = 7 pairs, zh→de = 4, zh→zh = 2 (visible in the
   figure). The Limitations §"Scale" already says "read sparse cells as indicative"; good,
   keep it and do **not** make any single-cell claim load-bearing. (b) The "English easiest
   target" sentence (B1) is the one design-unsound claim here — it is a pooled-column
   artifact that the shown (best-model) figure contradicts. Fix per B1.

3. **MT-of-question null (Fig 5).** Correctly framed. The draft says "statistically
   *insignificant*", "we read this as a *null* result", "does not significantly hurt and we
   do not claim it helps." This is exactly the right framing for p=0.13 (the role file's
   explicit check). `paired_n = 120` (patent×model) is an adequate N for the paired test.
   **Leave alone.** One optional strengthener: the *pooled* (unpaired) gap is −0.078 and the
   paired is −0.044; the paper correctly uses the paired number (controls for patent).

4. **Mate-retrieval (Fig 6–7).** Sound; the "15% never in top-1000" and "median rank 5"
   both trace. Note median first-foreign rank ranges 5 (egemma) → 559 (gte-base), so
   "most models do far worse" (line ~433) is supported by the per-model column.

5. **RBO consistency (Fig ag1, cp_fig08).** Sound. Caveat: RBO and overlap@10 are distinct;
   the abstract/intro gloss "share fewer than four of their top-10" is really **overlap@10
   = 0.387** (≈3.9/10), not RBO = 0.39 — they happen to coincide numerically for
   embeddinggemma (overlap10 0.3867 vs rbo 0.3867 in `round01/summary.json`), so the gloss
   is *accidentally* exact here but the two metrics are not the same quantity. Low risk;
   optionally say "share fewer than four of their top-10 (overlap@10 ≈ 0.39)".

6. **Confusion / separability (Fig ag2, ag8, cp_fig11).** All values trace exactly. The
   causal reading "separability deficit ⇒ a monolingual re-ranker cannot fix it" is a
   *correlational* result (r=+0.96 across 9 models, n=9) stated as a mechanism. It is framed
   as "the falsifiable claim that …" (C3), which is honest, but n=9 models is a small basis
   for r=+0.96 — see T5.

7. **Concept-lens recall correctly flagged.** The draft says the concept lens "is capped by
   its ∼109 positives and must not be read as recall quality" and "The per-publication lens
   is the one we read as a quality score." This is the exact flag the role file required;
   `round03` confirms concept-lens R@10 is *mechanically* capped. **Verified-correct.**

8. **MRS / CLIR-MRS composites.** Both definitions in §Metrics match the source
   (`round10` raw axes; the `cap × (0.5+0.5·rob)` form). These are bespoke, min–max
   normalized composites — fine as a *deployment* score, but the writer should keep the
   honest hedge already present ("capability is the spine and robustness modulates it by
   ±50%") and not over-claim that MRS is a universal metric.

## Overlooked / confounds / threats-to-validity (with the minimal fix each)

**T1 — Home-advantage vs gold-availability confound (PARTIALLY HANDLED, strengthen).**
The Analysis already says English's lead is "mostly *where the gold lives*, not encoder
skill" and gives 42% vs 8–10%. Good. But the *headline* abstract/intro still lead with
"home advantage reaches +0.55" as if it were an encoder property. **Minimal fix:** one
clause in the abstract or §Results tying the +0.55 to the availability confound that
Analysis later resolves — e.g. "(much of which is a gold-availability artifact, §6)".
Otherwise a reviewer reads §Results and §Analysis as contradicting each other.

**T2 — English-target asymmetry as corpus composition, not encoder behavior (UNHANDLED).**
The directional anisotropy (en→de hard, English/French strong targets) is *driven by the
corpus language mix* — `round06.corpus_base_share` is en 0.46, fr 0.37, es 0.09, de 0.07,
zh 0.004. zh is 0.4% of the corpus, so zh-target cells are tiny and noisy and the "anisotropy"
is largely "there are almost no German/Chinese documents to retrieve." **Minimal fix:** add
one sentence to §Results or Limitations: "directional asymmetry partly reflects corpus
language composition (en 46% / zh 0.4% of documents), not only encoder behavior." This also
defends against the contamination/composition reviewer the Related Work cites.

**T3 — "oracle/RRF headroom" interpretation (HANDLED, keep tight).** The draft handles the
"don't ensemble" vs "headroom is real" tension correctly and together (oracle 0.61 / 88%,
RRF negative). The numbers trace. Keep the two facts in the same paragraph as written; do
not let a later edit split them. No fix needed.

**T4 — Parallel-gold equivalence (family vs MT) — the Equivalence-Audit concern (UNHANDLED
as a *measured* result; honestly deferred).** The paper's central honesty claim is "gold is
genuinely parallel human-translated patents, never publication_number equivalence." The
*design* defends this (Related Work patent-family paragraph; §Benchmarks "Honesty by
design"). But there is **no on-disk equivalence audit** quantifying that the parallel golds
are truly equivalent — it is asserted by construction. The draft already lists this under
Limitations ("claim-level patent equivalence is not lay-annotatable … remains future work")
and Story risk #2, so it is **honestly deferred, not hidden**. **Minimal fix:** none
required for correctness; but consider adding `needs_eval.md` item for a small
expert-annotated equivalence spot-check, since a hostile reviewer will ask "how do you know
your parallel golds are equivalent?" and "by construction" is the only current answer.

**T5 — Small-n correlations stated as mechanisms (UNHANDLED).** Several headline r's are
across **n=9 models** (r(AUC,CLIR@10)=+0.96; r(over-rep,CLIR)=−0.60) or **n=9 language-pair
points** (r(home adv,RBO)=−0.85/−0.87). With n=9, r=+0.96 is significant but the CI is wide
and one outlier (gte-base / e5, which collapse) drives much of the spread. **Minimal fix:**
report these as "across the nine models" (the draft mostly does) and, for the load-bearing
+0.96, add "(n=9 models; driven in part by the two collapsed encoders)" or show the scatter
already exists in `cp_fig11`. Do not upgrade these to causal language beyond the current
"falsifiable claim" hedge.

**T6 — Baseline run is a different corpus; ensure it is never cited as a number.** The
baseline `20260601-235117_137questions` uses a **1,110-doc** corpus and reports bge-m3
R@10 = 0.717 — wildly higher than the 0.476 in the key_findings (which uses the 23,487-doc
shared corpus). The paper correctly cites **only** the key_findings numbers and never the
baseline, so there is no leak today. **Minimal fix:** none needed, but flag for the writer:
do not pull any number from `20260601-235117_137questions/` into the paper — it is a stale
small-corpus run and would silently inflate recall.

**T7 — Two RBO ceilings must stay separated (HANDLED).** 0.39 (alias) and 0.19 (CP) are
correctly attributed to their own benchmarks throughout. Keep them separate; never average.
(Only the "best model reaches 0.19" wording needs the B2 fix.)
