\begin{abstract}
Chemical knowledge is produced, searched, and reused across language boundaries:
a technical question may be asked in one language while the strongest evidence
appears in another. Yet multilingual chemistry retrieval has little clean public
evaluation data, so models are often judged by aggregate recall even when that
number hides the failure that matters most: retrieving the right evidence across
languages rather than an easier same-language match. We address this gap with a
multilingual chemistry QAC benchmark and generation pipeline for cross-lingual
retrieval evaluation. Patents provide the benchmark substrate in this work
because multilingual publication variants and chemistry ontologies make it
possible to construct controlled relevance judgments and chemically plausible
hard negatives. The pipeline extracts chemistry-relevant technical text,
generates multiple question-answer-context candidates with an LLM, grades them
for faithfulness and retrieval quality, and validates the automated grading
against human annotation. We export the resulting data in retrieval-ready form
with corpus, queries, qrels, and QAC records, enabling evaluation by query
language, document language, same-language versus cross-language relevance, and
chemically confusable hard negatives. Across evaluated multilingual embedding
models, average performance masks systematic cross-language weakness: models can
prefer same-language or chemically similar but irrelevant documents over the
intended evidence. The benchmark and pipeline provide a practical framework for
building and auditing specialized multilingual retrieval evaluations in
high-trust technical domains. Data and code are available on
\href{https://huggingface.co/datasets/MehdiAstaraki/multi-lingual-qac-chem-patents}{Hugging Face}
and
\href{https://github.com/MohammadKhodadad/Multi-Lingual-QAC}{GitHub}.
\end{abstract}
