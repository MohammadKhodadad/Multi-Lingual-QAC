Technical chemistry search rarely respects a single language boundary: a chemist may query in English, German, French, or Spanish, while the strongest evidence resides in a translated patent family, a regional filing, or a document whose language differs from that of the query. And it is not only a translation
problem, chemistry retrieval must also separate a compound from its chemically similar
but wrong neighbours. A retriever for a high-trust workflow such as patent search or
RAG therefore has to do two hard things at once: cross the language barrier and resist
chemical confusability.

We present X-ChemIR, a patent-grounded benchmark suite that measures both. It pairs two
multilingual chemistry question-answer-context (QAC) retrieval benchmarks built from
Google Patents and EPO patent data, patent families carry the same invention in
several languages at chemistry-rich scale \citep{clefip2013}, with an auxiliary
alias-graph stress test whose hard negatives are chemically confusable neighbours, and
evaluates eight off-the-shelf multilingual embedding models as dense retrievers. The
suite is built to expose what a single averaged Recall@$k$ hides: even the best model
leaves nearly half of the cross-language evidence out of the top-10, a same-language
home advantage persists in every query language, and a confusable look-alike out-ranks
the right document on $14$--$48\%$ of alias-graph queries. Existing multilingual
benchmarks do not surface this, as they neither control language variants nor supply
chemically plausible hard negatives.

This is therefore an evaluation-framework paper. Our
contributions are: (1) two patent-grounded multilingual chemistry-patent retrieval
benchmarks (Google Patents and EPO) with query/document-language metadata, plus an
auxiliary alias-graph confusability stress test; (2) a reproducible LLM-assisted QAC
generation and human-audit pipeline; (3) a robustness-metric family reported next to
recall-same- versus cross-language relevance (LT), cross-lingual coverage depth
($k^\star$), cross-lingual ranking consistency (RBO), and three position based metrics on cross lingual gold (XRC, RRC, ARI); (4) an evaluation of eight multilingual embedders that
quantifies the cross-lingual collapse and the chemical-confusion failure.
